Seven replacement emails for the broker sequence. Nothing here is live: the templates in the database are untouched until you say go. Rendered below exactly as a recipient sees them, merged for a sample broker in Illinois.

The diagnosis. Brokers click more than carriers and convert less, which is not a copy problem — it is an ask problem. Six of the seven old emails asked a broker who has never heard of LoadBoot to create an account, and never said what the account actually gives them. The zero-setup path — cc loads@loadboot.com on a list they already send — appeared once, as a footnote under a second button on day 1.

What changed. Every email now carries both doors: the cc address as a highlighted block (nothing to set up) and the free account as the one button, so both asks are present but there is still a single click target. And every email states plainly what the free account includes — ten items on days 1, 5 and 7, a one-paragraph version on the rest — with the reason it can be free: LoadBoot is funded by the flat 5% carrier-side dispatch fee, so the broker is never billed. Every claim is taken from the live site (free-load-board-for-brokers.html and pricing.html); nothing is invented.

Before you say go

1. The sign-off. All seven sign “LoadBoot · Broker Desk”. A real first name reads better and replies better. Tell me the name and I will set it.

2. Two of the ten free-list items need your yes. “Claims desk and document collection” and “the phone app” both come off the live site, but confirm a broker really can use them today — a promise a new signup cannot find is worse than not making it.

3. hello@ is not routed inbound. Reply rate on this sequence will read 0 whatever the copy does, because broker replies never reach the database — they only land in your inbox. Routing hello@ through the inbound-mail webhook is the one change that makes the next version measurable.

4. Honesty check. Day 7 says out loud that LoadBoot is a young platform. That is deliberate and it is true. Say the word and I will cut it — but it is the line most likely to earn a reply.
