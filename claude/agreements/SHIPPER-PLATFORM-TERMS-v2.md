## 2. What LoadBoot is — and what it is not

2.1 **LoadBoot is software and an online marketplace.** It lets shippers, licensed freight brokers and FMCSA-authorized motor carriers find each other and share information. It also helps them keep their shipment records: tracking, documents, confirmations and ratings.

2.2 **LoadBoot is not a broker, a freight forwarder or a motor carrier.** It holds no broker or carrier operating authority. It is **not a party** to any contract for transportation between you and a carrier, or between you and a broker.

2.3 LoadBoot **does not**:
- (a) set, suggest as binding, or negotiate the rate for your freight;
- (b) choose, assign or approve a carrier or a broker for your freight;
- (c) collect, hold, pay or pass along freight charges;
- (d) receive any part of the freight charge from you. (Carriers that choose LoadBoot's dispatch service pay LoadBoot a separate service fee under their own agreement. See Section 7.4.)

LoadBoot staff **review** each load before it is posted to carriers (Section 4.4). They may **block** a load, a shipper, a carrier or a broker to prevent fraud or for safety. Reviewing and blocking are the only decisions LoadBoot makes about a shipment.

2.4 Rate estimates and "recommended carriers" are **information only**. You decide.

2.5 **LoadBoot dispatch acts only for carriers.** Some carriers use LoadBoot's dispatch service. A LoadBoot dispatcher then acts only as that carrier's agent, under the carrier's own agreement with LoadBoot, never for you. On your loads a LoadBoot dispatcher:
- may ask to book your load **at the rate you posted**;
- does **not** negotiate the rate with you, counter your offers, or message you about the rate;
- cannot accept the Master Shipper–Carrier Transportation Terms for a carrier. The carrier accepts them itself.

## 3. Two ways to ship

3.1 **Direct to carrier.**
- You post the load and set the rate.
- Carriers ask to book it, and **you choose and accept the carrier**.
- Your contract for that load is with the carrier. It is made of the **Master Shipper–Carrier Transportation Terms**, which you sign once, plus the **Rate Agreement** for that load, which you and the carrier both accept.
- You pay the carrier, or the carrier's factoring company, **directly**.

3.2 **Tender to a broker.**
- You send a load tender to a licensed broker of **your** choice.
- LoadBoot shows only brokers with active FMCSA broker authority and a $75,000 surety bond or trust fund on file (49 U.S.C. 13906; 49 CFR 387.307).
- Your contract for that load is with the broker, on the broker's terms.

## 4. Your verification

4.1 You agree to give true, complete and current information about your company: legal entity, EIN, address, authorized signer, billing and cargo details. You will update it when it changes.

4.2 You agree that we may check that information to protect carriers and brokers from impersonation and fraud. Checks may include:

- public SEC filings;
- a telephone **call-back to a number we find ourselves** (for example from a state registry, your website or a public listing), not the number given at signup.

4.3 **Until your verification opens a lane, nothing you post reaches carriers or brokers.**

4.4 **Staff review before a load is posted.** When you post a load to carriers, it is first checked by LoadBoot staff. Carriers see it only after staff post it. In that review:
- (a) staff check only for fraud, safety and completeness — for example, that the load comes from your verified company, that the pickup date has not passed, and that the hazmat, food or high-value details are there when the load needs them;
- (b) staff post the load **exactly as you submitted it**. They do not change your rate, your rate card or any other term, and they do not choose, rank or suggest a carrier. If you named a carrier for a direct offer, the offer goes to that carrier at your rate;
- (c) staff may decline to post a load. The load then shows as declined in your portal, and you may submit a corrected load;
- (d) the review is not a check of any carrier, of the rate, or of the lawfulness of your shipment. It does not reduce your obligations under Section 5, and LoadBoot does not promise that a load will be reviewed by a particular time.

4.5 **New-shipper limits apply.** A new shipper may have a limited number of open loads and a limited cargo value per load. The limits are lifted once carriers confirm payment on a set number of loads. The current limits are shown in your portal.

4.6 We may place your account on hold if information is false or cannot be confirmed, or if a call-back does not confirm your company. While on hold, nothing reaches carriers or brokers.

## 5. Your obligations as a shipper

5.1 **Accurate declarations.** For every load, the weight, commodity, piece count, value and special requirements you give will be accurate. You are responsible for fines, losses or claims caused by your own mis-declaration.

5.2 **Hazardous materials.** If you offer hazardous materials:
- you comply with the Hazardous Materials Regulations, 49 CFR Parts 171–180;
- this includes proper shipping papers (Part 172, Subpart C) and a 24-hour emergency response telephone number (49 CFR 172.604);
- it also includes PHMSA registration where required (49 CFR 107.601);
- your employees who handle hazmat are trained as required by 49 CFR 172.704.

5.3 **Food.** If you ship human or animal food covered by the FDA Sanitary Transportation rule, you give the carrier your sanitary and temperature requirements **in writing**, as 21 CFR 1.908(b) requires.

5.4 **No coercion of drivers.** You will not coerce any driver to operate in violation of federal safety, hours-of-service or hazmat rules (49 CFR 390.6).

5.5 **Loading and unloading.** Where you require loading or unloading help, you provide it or pay for it (49 U.S.C. 14103). Your lumper and detention rules for each location are stated in your LoadBoot address book and in the Rate Agreement.

5.6 **Your locations.** The dock hours, appointment rules and detention terms you publish are accurate.
