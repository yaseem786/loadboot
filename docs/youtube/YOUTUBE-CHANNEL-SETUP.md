# LoadBoot YouTube channel — setup pack (carriers · brokers · shippers · dispatchers)

## Can you run it from Pakistan?

Yes, and there is nothing to work around.

**Creating the channel:** no country restriction. **Who sees it:** decided by *viewer* signals — what
they searched, what they have watched, their language and country — not by where the channel was
made. English video, US-trucking keywords, US audience, whether it was uploaded from Lahore or Dallas.

**Monetization:** the Partner Program *is* available in Pakistan, but ignore it. At realistic year-one
view counts, ad revenue is a few dollars a month; one carrier signup is worth more than every ad you
would ever run. Do not chase 1,000 subs / 4,000 hours — chase the comment that says "how do I sign up".

**The one setting that actually matters:** default language **English (United States)**. It drives how
captions and metadata are read and it is a real ranking input. Channel country is not — leave it accurate.

## The four audiences, and what each one is actually searching

This is the whole SEO plan. Do not write a video without knowing which row it belongs to.

| Audience | What they type into YouTube | Where they must land |
|---|---|---|
| **Carrier / owner-operator** | "detention pay trucking", "how to get loads with new authority", "owner operator profit" | `/carriers.html`, `/create-carrier-account.html`, `/owner-operator-dispatch.html`, `/new-authority-dispatch.html` |
| **Dispatcher** (highest commercial intent) | "how much does a truck dispatcher cost", "truck dispatcher vs freight broker", "do I need a dispatcher" | `/us-truck-dispatcher.html`, `/how-much-does-a-truck-dispatcher-cost.html`, `/truck-dispatcher-vs-freight-broker.html`, `/do-new-authority-carriers-need-a-dispatcher.html` |
| **Broker** | "free load board for brokers", "how to vet a carrier", "post loads" | `/brokers.html`, `/free-load-board-for-brokers.html`, `/create-broker-account.html` |
| **Shipper** | "ship direct to carrier", "how to ship without a broker", "freight tracking" | `/shipper-solutions.html`, `/ship-direct-to-carrier.html`, `/how-to-ship-without-a-broker.html`, `/create-shipper-account.html` |

The dispatcher row is the one most people would skip, and it is the most valuable: "how much does a
truck dispatcher cost" is pure buying intent from someone who has already decided they need help. You
already have the page that answers it. A video pointed at that page is the highest-converting thing
on this list.

### Role-specific or general? Role-specific. Always.

Videos compete in **search**, and search is a query. "Detention pay: the 5 rules" matches something a
person types. "Everything about accessorials" matches nothing — nobody types that. A general video has
no door for anyone to come in through. The only general video on this channel is the trailer.

**One channel, not four.** Four channels means four times the work and three starved channels. But one
channel carries a real cost you should understand: YouTube suggests by **subscriber overlap**. If 70%
of your subscribers are carriers and you post a shipper video, YouTube shows it to carriers, they don't
watch, and the video dies. That is the actual price of mixing audiences — not brand confusion, buried
videos.

### The four audiences are NOT equal on YouTube

This is the part that changes what you produce:

| Audience | Presence on YouTube | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Carriers / owner-operators | Very large. Truckers watch hours of YouTube. | **~70% of production.** The volume lives here. |
| Dispatchers | Smaller, very high intent — and they watch carrier content anyway. | **~20%.** Largely free on the back of carrier videos. |
| Brokers | Thin. Brokers are on LinkedIn, not YouTube. | **~10%.** |
| Shippers | Effectively absent for this topic. Logistics managers research on Google and LinkedIn. | **0% for YouTube reach.** |

**So do not make shipper videos for YouTube discovery.** Serve shippers through the website and LinkedIn.

### The move that resolves the tension

Broker and shipper videos still get made — but their job is different:

- **Carrier + dispatcher videos → made for YouTube.** Titled for search, thumbnail optimised, promoted,
  comments worked. These are the ones chasing reach.
- **Broker + shipper videos → made for the website.** Their job is to sit on `brokers.html`,
  `free-load-board-for-brokers.html`, `shipper-solutions.html` and `ship-direct-to-carrier.html` so
  those pages become eligible for Google's video-rich result. Public on YouTube (an unlisted video
  cannot be embedded with schema), but not promoted, and not posted into the subscriber feed if it
  would break momentum on a carrier video.

Same production pipeline, different success metric. Judge a carrier video on YouTube impressions and a
shipper video on whether its page moved in Google. Do not judge either by the other's numbers.

### Keeping the feed coherent

- Put the role in the title where it is not obvious: `... for Owner-Operators`, `... for Dispatchers`.
- Always add the video to its audience playlist — that is a grouping signal the algorithm uses.
- Do not publish a broker video into the 48 hours after a carrier video. Let the carrier video finish
  its run first.

Note there is deliberately **no dispatcher signup** — dispatch is what LoadBoot *provides* at flat 5%.
Independent dispatchers go to `/agents.html` (1% referral) or `/careers.html` (US Dispatcher role).
Do not invent a "dispatcher account" in any copy.

channels* → *Create a new channel*. A Brand Account takes multiple managers and can be transferred.
A channel welded to your personal Google login cannot, without pain.

**2. Name / handle.** `LoadBoot` · `@loadboot` (fallback `@loadbootdispatch` — short, no numbers).

**3. Branding tab.** Avatar, banner, watermark from the table above.

read as a topic signal, and giving four audiences equal billing turns that signal to mush.

## Channel layout — one section per audience

Sections, top to bottom. Do not create a section you cannot fill; an empty playlist reads as abandoned.

1. **Channel trailer** — the detention video. Strongest thing you have, and it demonstrates the product
   without pitching it.
2. **Get paid what you're owed** *(carriers — the spine of the channel)* — detention, TONU, layover,
   lumper. Each maps to a page you already rank for.
3. **What dispatch should cost** *(dispatchers — highest commercial intent)* — dispatcher cost,
   dispatcher vs broker, does a new authority need one.
4. **Running a new authority** *(carriers)* — first loads, cost per mile, staying compliant.
5. **Read the paperwork** *(everyone)* — rate confirmations, BOL/POD, broker setup packets.
6. **For brokers & shippers** *(one shared section, kept small on purpose)* — these videos exist mainly
   to embed on their pages. Do not pad this out to look symmetrical with the carrier sections.
7. Uploads.

## What actually grows this without ad spend

**Search beats browse for every one of these four.** Somebody typing "how much does a truck dispatcher
cost" is worth fifty idle scrollers. Title for the question, not for the clever line.

**Each video ships with its page.** You already have the pages — `detention-pay-policy.html`,
`us-truck-dispatcher.html`, `how-much-does-a-truck-dispatcher-cost.html`,
`truck-dispatcher-vs-freight-broker.html`, `free-load-board-for-brokers.html`,
`ship-direct-to-carrier.html`. Video embeds on the page, page links the video. The page ranks in
Google, the video ranks in YouTube, each feeds the other. Almost nobody in this niche does both halves.

**One audience per video, always.** A video that tries to talk to carriers and shippers at once ranks
for neither. Pick the row, write to that row, and put the other three in the description links.

**Answer every comment in the first 48 hours.** Cheapest retention signal there is, and the one most
channels skip.

**The description links convert, not the video.** Nobody signs up because a video was good. They sign
up because the first line told them exactly where to go and it cost nothing to try.
