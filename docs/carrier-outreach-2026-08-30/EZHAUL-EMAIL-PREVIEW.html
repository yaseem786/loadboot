What happens the moment that certificate clears

Three things open up at once. None of them need a phone call.

1  ·  You post your truck, and loads come to you
On the Load Board you tell us where the truck is, where it is empty from, how far you will run and your minimum rate, then hit Post truck. We tell you immediately how many loads on the board already match, and every new load that fits alerts you from then on. Your dispatcher sees the same thing and can start working the lane without asking you where you are.

One rule worth knowing now, so it does not surprise you later: a truck can only be posted if its VIN is on your certificate of insurance, and it needs that VIN saved under Fleet. Post a truck that is not on the policy schedule and the portal stops you rather than putting you in front of a broker who would find out at pickup. That is why the message to your agent above asks for the VINs on the schedule — get it right on this reissue and you never have to touch it again.
2  ·  You control what you get offered
Under Account → Dispatch you set your minimum rate per mile, the most deadhead you will run, states you will not go to, whether weekends are in play, and where home is. Set these before the certificate lands, not after — the day loads open you want the board already filtered to work you would actually take, instead of scrolling past freight that was never right. The Online / Offline switch at the top of the portal is the same setting: flip it off and offers stop, without anyone having to phone you.
3  ·  Your profile is what brokers actually read
My Profile shows your live FMCSA record next to what you tell us about the equipment — trailer type, deck length, payload, whether you have straps, chains, tarps, a liftgate. Brokers use that to decide whether to release a load without a phone call. A thin profile is not neutral; it loses you loads quietly. Ten minutes here is worth more than any other ten minutes on the platform.

Getting paid — set this up before the first load, not after

You have two routes, and they are set up in the same place: Account → Payments.

If you are getting paid direct
Add a payout method with your bank name, account holder name, account number and routing number. The routing number is checked as you type, so a transposed digit is caught on the spot instead of two weeks later. The account holder name has to match EZHAUL LLC — a personal account in your own name is the single most common reason a first settlement stalls. A person reviews the details before any money moves, and the numbers are masked once saved.
If you are factoring
Tell us the factoring company on the same screen and upload their Notice of Assignment under Documents — the NOA letter, as a PDF, on the factor’s letterhead. Without it we cannot legally direct your money to them, and a broker who pays the wrong party creates a mess that takes weeks to unwind. If you are still choosing a factor, say so in a reply and we will tell you what to watch for in the contract before you sign — the rate is rarely the part that costs you.
One thing on your FMCSA record worth fixing this week
Your MCS-150 currently shows zero power units and cargo carried as construction materials only. Neither blocks you with us, but both cost you real loads elsewhere: a lot of brokers auto-decline a for-hire carrier showing no trucks, and the cargo classification is the field they check before offering anything outside building materials. Filing an MCS-150 update is free, takes about ten minutes at motus.dot.gov, and it is the cheapest thing you can do this month to widen what you get offered. Reply if you want us to walk you through the two fields that matter.
